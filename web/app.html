Help: what everything means

  

    
Point at a bar, a speed, a verdict or a score in the list to see a short explanation with that model's own numbers. Press ? to open this window, Esc to close it. The list at the left jumps to a part; its search box shows only the rows that contain your words.


    
The memory bar

    
The thin bar in the list shows if a model fits in your graphics card's memory (VRAM). For a model that fits, the whole bar is the VRAM you can use for models (your VRAM minus "Kept for Windows").

    

      
Grey bar
The part that sits in VRAM: model file, KV cache and engine together. Fast. The % at the right of the line below it is how much of your usable VRAM that is.

      
Amber end
The grey bar ends in solid amber when less than 12 % of the usable VRAM stays free: it runs, but a longer context or other programs can push it over.

      
Orange stripes
The model does not fit in VRAM. The rest goes to system RAM, which is often about 10 times slower, so the model runs slower. Then the whole bar is everything the model needs, to scale: grey = the part in VRAM (your VRAM is full), orange stripes = the part in RAM. The numbers below the bar give both, for example "23.0 GB VRAM + 64.6 GB RAM"; there is no % then. With no GPU the bar is fully striped: everything is in RAM. When the list is narrower than about 900 px (a small screen or a wide detail panel), the words drop out and the numbers stay: "23.0 GB + 64.6 GB", and "16.3 GB" instead of "16.3 GB of 23.0 GB" (the % at the right gives the share).

      
Empty part
VRAM that stays free.

    

    
The big bar in the detail panel (tab Overview) splits it further, with other colours: purple is the model file, and grey is only the engine. For a model that fits in VRAM, the big bar is your usable VRAM; for one that does not, it is the whole memory pool (VRAM + RAM), with "VRAM ends at …" below it.

    

      
Model file
The weights of the model (the .gguf file), in VRAM. For a mixture of experts that keeps its experts in RAM (MoE offload): everything in VRAM (the active experts, KV cache and engine).

      
Orange stripes (big bar)
The part of the model file in system RAM (slower). MoE offload: the inactive experts in RAM.

      
KV cache
Memory for the context (the text the model remembers). It grows with "Context length". KV cache Q8 or Q4 makes it smaller.

      
Engine + other
Buffers of llama.cpp / LM Studio.

      
Thin line
Where your VRAM ends, when the model is bigger than VRAM.

    


    
Verdicts and "Runs on"

    

      
Runs well
Fits fully in VRAM with room to spare.

      
Runs, but tight
Fits in VRAM, but with little room left. A longer context or another program can push it over.

      
Runs, but slower
Part of the model runs from system RAM, or there is no GPU.

      
Won't fit
VRAM and RAM together are too small. In the list such a row is a little paler (hidden by default: the tile Won't fit shows them).

      
GPU
Everything in VRAM. The list shows no line under the verdict then.

      
CPU+GPU
Part in VRAM, part in system RAM.

      
MoE offload
A "mixture of experts" model: it has many experts but uses only a few per word. The unused experts wait in system RAM, so even a very big model can run, more slowly.

      
CPU
Everything from system RAM: there is no GPU, or llmfit runs this kind of model on the CPU only (for example a BitNet model). The memory label then says "… in RAM".

    

    
Above the list

    

      
Top picks for this PC
Three suggestions that follow your filters (but not the four tiles), from the models that fit, without embedding models and "size uncertain" rows: Best (the highest score that still gives 12+ tok/s), Quality (the best quality score at reading pace or faster) and Fastest (the quickest model with a quality score of 60 or more), each with its verdict, file and speed. Point at a name or a label for its rule. View details opens the model; Add to battle puts it in Battle. Closed (the start), they are one slim strip with the three names (click a name to open it); the arrow at its right, or the sparkle button next to Sort, shows them as cards; Hide folds the cards back into the strip.

      
The four tiles
The segments at the left of the toolbar count each verdict. Click one to show or hide those models; a hidden one is grey and says "hidden". Won't fit is hidden at the start (and again after Defaults). With an upgrade on, a small purple "+N" next to the number says how many of them fit only thanks to the upgrade.

      
Count line
At the right of the filter chips: how many models are shown, how many the tiles hide, how many llmfit analysed, and how long that took.

      
Filter chips
Every active filter is a chip: click its × to remove it. Clear all shows every analysed model; Defaults gives the start values.

      
Export CSV
The arrow-up button right of Sort, between the sparkle (Top picks) and the (i): saves the shown list as a CSV file (for a spreadsheet).

      
Legend
The (i) button next to Sort: opens this help at "The memory bar".

      
Sort
The list next to the arrows icon: best match, fastest, smallest, largest, longest usable context, newest, most downloaded, or name.

    


    
The columns

    

      
Model
The name (↗ opens it on Hugging Face), its labels and icons (see below), the publisher and the number of parameters: B = billion, M = million. More parameters usually means smarter, but bigger and slower. The star makes it a favorite; the box selects it for compare or battle. Both show when you point at a row, and stay on the chosen row, on a starred model and on a ticked box.

      
Best file
The quantization (file version) llmfit picks for this PC. Q8_0 is close to the original quality; Q6, Q5, Q4, Q3, Q2 are smaller and faster, but less precise. F16/BF16 = original size. When the list area is narrow (below about 830 px: a laptop screen, or a wide detail panel), this column is left out; the detail panel shows it.

      
Memory
The memory bar (see above), with the memory the model needs.

      
Speed
Tokens per second (a token is about ¾ of a word). A range, because it is an estimate. About 7 tok/s is reading pace. A model that won't fit shows "—": it has no speed on this PC. Very fast small models (1,000 tok/s and more) show only the numbers, from 10,000 tok/s in thousands ("13k–17k"), so they fit the column.

      
Verdict
How well it runs here. Under it, where it runs: CPU+GPU, MoE offload or CPU. No line means GPU (everything in VRAM).

      
Score
llmfit's match for this PC, 0 to 100, built from quality, speed, fit and context. On a very narrow list (below about 570 px, a small laptop screen) this column is left out; the verdict stays.

      
Chosen row
The model you clicked (or opened with Show or View details) has a purple tint and a glowing purple bar at its left edge; its details are on the right.

    


    
Labels and icons

    

      
 Vision
The model can read images (photos, screenshots). It does not make images.

      
 Tool use
The model can call functions, so AI agents can use it.

      
 Reasoning
The model thinks step by step before it answers. "(from the model name)" means only the name says so (for example "Thinking" or "R1"), not llmfit's list.

      
NEW
Released in the last 60 days: the date this repo was made on Hugging Face. A new re-upload of an old model does not make the old model new.

      
MTP
At the top of the detail panel (and in My models), not in the list. Multi-token prediction: the model has extra layers that guess the next few tokens at once. "MTP (from the name)": only the name says so; a grey "MTP (not in this file)": the model has MTP layers (config.json), but its GGUF file has none, because the conversion left them out; look for a GGUF with "MTP" in the name. In LM Studio, speculative decoding with MTP can then answer faster with the same quality. TensorTailor reads it from the GGUF header of the file (tab Files), else from config.json, else from the name ("from the name"). For the MTP labels in My models, see "My models" below.

      
in LM Studio
You already have this model in LM Studio.

      
live
Not in llmfit's list: fetched live from Hugging Face (Tools → Catalog, or a pasted link). Models in your LM Studio that llmfit's list does not have are fetched like this by themselves, so the filter "Already in LM Studio" shows all of them. At the top of the detail panel the same label is a green "live from Hugging Face"; "from LM Studio" (below) is green there too.

      
from LM Studio
In your LM Studio, but not in llmfit's list, and llmfit could not read it from Hugging Face. The row is made from what LM Studio and the model file's header say (parameters, quant, layers, context); llmfit's formulas give the verdict. Rougher than a model from llmfit's list. Its name is the Hugging Face repo of its folder when there is one; else it starts with lmstudio/: then no Hugging Face page is known, its ↗ link searches Hugging Face for the name, and it is counted apart from the live models (Tools → Catalog).

      
Kept, not readable
In Tools → Catalog: fetched models (live or from LM Studio) that this version of the engine could not read, for example after an llmfit update changed the row format. They are not in the list, but they are kept (not deleted) and tried again each time the page opens. Remove fetched models removes them too.

      
size uncertain
The name shows already-compressed weights that are not GGUF (EXL2/EXL3, bnb, AWQ, GPTQ, MLX, vllm, "9bit" …), or says far more parameters than llmfit counts (for example 27B in the name, 10.3B counted). llmfit then counted the compressed weights, so its memory and speed are too optimistic. These rows come after all other models that fit (before Won't fit) and are never a top pick; the Overview tab says why. Look for a GGUF copy of the original model instead.

      
gated
Gated on Hugging Face: accept the terms on the model page first, then set your Hugging Face token (the key button at the top right, or Tools → Hugging Face token) to download the files. A repo with an open license can be gated too.

      
license (grey)
Not a plain open license (MIT, Apache): Llama, Gemma, non-commercial or research licenses have their own terms. Read them on the model page. Not gated: the files download without a token.

      
license (amber)
The same kind of license, but not checked yet: the guess comes from the license name. Open the model and TensorTailor asks Hugging Face; the label then becomes gated or a grey license.

      
Where "gated" comes from
The page carries Hugging Face's list of gated models from the day it was built (the weekly release makes a fresh one). When you open a model, TensorTailor also asks Hugging Face live and remembers that answer for 7 days. Point at a label to see where its answer comes from. The filter No token needed hides the gated models.

      
MoE
Mixture of experts (see MoE offload).

      
A full label line
When a row in the list has more labels than room, it leaves out whole labels, the least important first: license, then live (or from LM Studio), measured and NEW, then the small icons (vision, tool use, reasoning). Point at the line to see which; the detail panel shows them all. The number of parameters always stays. The maker's name may end in "…"; in a very full line it is left out, and on a very narrow list the warnings (in LM Studio, gated, vLLM, size uncertain) go last. A memory label that does not fit leaves out its words: "23.0 GB + 63.8 GB".

      
with upgrade
At the top of the detail panel when "What if I upgrade?" is on: the verdict, the numbers and the Run commands are for the upgraded PC. The Run tab says so too; switch the upgrade off for commands that fit your PC as it is now.

      
upgrade
With the upgrade you switched on under "What if I upgrade?", this model fits fully in VRAM (runs well or runs, but tight); on your PC as it is now, it runs slower or does not fit.

      
measured
The speed comes from real measurements on similar hardware (llmfit's own community results), not from an estimate: the list and the detail panel show the measured number (point at the speed for the number of runs and llmfit's formula estimate). Only PCs with the same CPU and GPU as a community result get it. Your own measurements on this PC are shown separately: see "My models: Measure".

      
vLLM and others
The model needs another engine than llama.cpp (the label names it: vLLM or MLX); LM Studio on Windows cannot run it.

    


    
Words in model names

    
Many models on Hugging Face are copies of a known model that someone changed. The name tells how. For some of these words (abliterated, Heretic, uncensored, derestricted, NSFW, Dolphin, role-play, base, distill, merge), the detail panel (tab Overview) shows a line Name says. It reads only the name, not the model. A changed copy has the same size and speed as the original, so TensorTailor's numbers stay the same.

    
Fewer refusals ("uncensored")

    

      
Why models refuse
After the main training, a model is taught to say no to harmful requests. Sometimes it also refuses normal questions (security, medicine, history, dark stories). The versions below take most of those refusals out.

      
uncensored · decensored · unfiltered
The umbrella word: the model refuses little or nothing. The name does not say how. Often it was trained again on data without refusals; sometimes it is abliterated.

      
abliterated · obliterated
The refusals were cut out of the weights, without new training. Researchers found (2024) that refusing is mostly one "direction" inside the model; abliteration removes that direction.

      
Heretic
A free tool (by p-e-w, since late 2025) that does abliteration by itself. It looks for the setting with the fewest refusals and the least damage. Its model cards often give two numbers: refusals (for example 3/100) and KL divergence (how far the answers moved from the original; lower is closer to the original).

      
derestricted · norm-preserving
A newer, gentler kind of abliteration: it keeps the size of the other weights the same, so the model loses less.

      
Dolphin
A known series of models trained again on data with the refusals taken out.

      
RP · roleplay · NSFW
Trained further on stories, role-play or adult texts. Fewer refusals is a side effect; the goal is the writing style.

      
Good
Fewer refusals of normal requests, and no lectures.

      
Not so good
Often a little less accurate than the original (tests show it most in math and facts). It does not know more: removing refusals adds no knowledge, so it makes up what it never learned. The quality varies a lot, because anyone can upload one. You are responsible for what you do with it, and the license of the original model still holds.

      
How to choose
Start from a popular original model and a known publisher, read the model card (refusals, KL divergence), and test it next to the original with your own questions. If it is not better for your task, you do not need it.

      
The filter
Hide uncensored versions (Filters, left) hides models whose name says abliterated (or obliterated), Heretic, uncensored (or decensored, unfiltered), derestricted or NSFW. It does not hide Dolphin or role-play models (they get a Name says line only), and it cannot see models that refuse less without such a word in the name.

    

    
Training and kind

    

      
Base
Only pre-trained: it continues text, but does not chat or follow instructions well. To chat, take the Instruct version.

      
Instruct · Chat · it
Trained to follow instructions and to chat ("-it" = instruction-tuned). This is what you usually want.

      
Thinking · R1
Thinks step by step before it answers: better at hard tasks, but slower, because the thinking costs tokens too (see the Reasoning icon).

      
Distill
A smaller model taught by a bigger one. For example DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B is Qwen 7B taught by DeepSeek R1. Good value, but not as good as the big model.

      
Fine-tune
A model trained further by someone else for one job: a language, a style or a task. The model card names the original; My models → Similar groups the ones you have.

      
Merge · slerp
Two or more models mixed into one. The results vary a lot.

      
Coder · VL · Embedding
Made for code; reads images (vision-language); turns text into numbers for search (it does not chat).

    

    
Size and file

    

      
8B · 70B
Billions of parameters (see "The columns").

      
A3B
A mixture of experts: "30B-A3B" has 30 billion parameters, of which 3 billion work per word. Fast for its size, but the whole model must fit in VRAM and RAM together (see MoE offload).

      
GGUF · Q4_K_M · Q8_0
GGUF is the file type LM Studio uses; Q4_K_M and the like are the quantizations (see "Best file").

      
i1 · imatrix · UD
Smarter quantization: an importance matrix (i1, imatrix) or Unsloth Dynamic (UD) keeps the important weights more precise, so a small file loses less quality.

    


    
Your hardware and settings (left)

    

      
The tag at the top
Where the hardware values come from: saved profile (kept in this browser), changed by you, from browser (Detect) or imported (Import…).

      
Usable for models
The line under the GPU name: your VRAM minus "Kept for Windows", for example "24 GB − 1 GB = 23.0 GB VRAM". With an upgrade on, a second line gives the upgraded VRAM and RAM.

      
GPU memory speed
At the top of Edit hardware: the speed of your graphics card's memory that llmfit uses for its speed estimate, "~936 GB/s (known card)" when the card is in llmfit's table. With no GPU: the speed of system RAM.

      
VRAM per GPU · Number of GPUs
Memory of one graphics card, and how many identical cards you have.

      
System RAM
Normal memory of the PC. Used when a model does not fit in VRAM.

      
Kept for Windows
VRAM that Windows and your screens use. Not available for models.

      
RAM speed
How fast system RAM is (GB/s). Sets the speed of the part that runs from RAM.

      
CPU cores
Used for the CPU part of a model.

      
Detect · Import…
Detect in browser fills in the GPU name the browser shows and its VRAM from a table (and sets Number of GPUs to 1 when it was 0). When that card's VRAM is not in the table and VRAM was 0, the VRAM box is selected: type the real number. Built-in graphics (Intel UHD or Iris Xe, AMD Radeon Graphics) share the system RAM, so they set Number of GPUs and VRAM to 0, as Import… does. The browser often cannot see a second graphics card next to built-in graphics: when WebGPU shows a chip by another maker, it says so and changes nothing; otherwise use Import…. When the browser shows no name (it draws in software, it hides the name, or WebGL is off), it says why and changes nothing. When you import or change the hardware while Detect still waits for the browser, your change wins. The browser cannot tell RAM or CPU cores, so those stay as they are. Import… gives a PowerShell line that reads your real hardware: GPU and VRAM (also without nvidia-smi), RAM, CPU cores, the VRAM in use now (for "Kept for Windows") and the free space on the drive with LM Studio's models folder. When PowerShell says "Copied…", the values are on the clipboard: click Paste from clipboard in the Import window (when the browser asks to see the clipboard, click Allow; for a file on disk it can ask each time), or press Ctrl+V in its box and click Use this hardware. When it cannot copy, it shows the text so you can copy it yourself. A wrong paste is said in red under the box.

      
What if I upgrade?
One more GPU of the same kind (only with a GPU), or 128 GB more RAM. Switch one on and the whole list, the tiles and the detail panel are calculated for the upgraded PC; models that fit fully in VRAM only with the upgrade get the label "upgrade".

      
What is it for?
Show only models for general use, coding, reasoning, chat, images + text or embedding.

      
Context length
How much text the model keeps in mind. Longer context = bigger KV cache = more memory.

      
KV cache
F16 is normal. Q8 halves the KV cache, Q4 makes it about a quarter, with a small loss in quality. The list always counts with F16. In the detail panel, Memory, the math line, the bar, "Which file fits?" (with its note) and the Files tab's "Fits here" use your choice, and the Plan tab counts with it; the verdict and the speed stay those of the list (F16), and one line under the math says so. When the smaller cache would let the model fit fully in VRAM (or fit at all), the numbers stay F16 and that line says what your choice gives.

      
Your choices are kept
Filters, what it is for, context length, KV cache, sort and the four verdict tiles are kept in this browser, also after a reload. Reset (Filters) puts the filters back to the start values. Your hardware is a short summary; Edit hardware opens the fields, Detect in browser and Import…. A saved hardware value that is not a valid number or name (other local pages can share this browser's storage) is replaced by the start value.

      
Storage full
When this browser's storage for TensorTailor is full, a message says that a change was not saved: it works until you close the page. Fetched models take the most room: Tools → Catalog → Remove fetched models frees it.

      
Search
Type a name or a publisher (for example bartowski). Several words find models that have all of them, in any order: qwen3 14b finds Qwen3-14B. From 2 letters, a line above the list shows how many matches your filters hide (Show all turns the filters off for this search; Filters on again turns them back on). Press Enter on an exact model name (or a pasted link) to open its Files tab; Enter on a word that matches nothing at all searches Hugging Face by itself. Search Hugging Face finds the publisher's models and models with the text in their name, and checks them with llmfit's own code (label live). Paste a Hugging Face link and press Enter to check one model (the line above the list reminds you).

    


    
Filters (left)

    

      
Language · Size
Only models for a language, or of a size (number of parameters).

      
Vision · Tool use · Reasoning
Only models with that skill (see the icons above). You can switch on more than one.

      
Runs in LM Studio (llama.cpp)
Only models that LM Studio can run. On by default. It also hides Llama 3.2 Vision (architecture mllama): llmfit lists it for llama.cpp, but llama.cpp does not run it (Ollama does). It also hides repos whose name shows already-compressed weights in another format (EXL3, bnb-4bit, AWQ, GPTQ, MLX, vllm …): LM Studio cannot load those as they are (label size uncertain).

      
Known GGUF download
Only models with a known GGUF copy on Hugging Face (the file type LM Studio uses).

      
Released in the last 60 days
Only new models (label NEW).

      
Only my favorites ★
Only the models you starred.

      
Already in LM Studio
Only the models you have in LM Studio (label "in LM Studio").

      
Open license (Apache, MIT)
Only models with a plain open license: Apache 2.0, MIT, BSD, CC-BY-4.0, OpenRAIL or BigScience.

      
No token needed
Hides the models that are gated on Hugging Face (label "gated").

      
Hide uncensored versions
Hides models whose name says abliterated (or obliterated), Heretic, uncensored (or decensored, unfiltered), derestricted or NSFW. Off by default. See "Words in model names".

      
Fits fully in VRAM
Only models that run fully on the graphics card.

      
Popular (10,000+ downloads)
Only models with 10,000 or more downloads. On by default. New or rare models can be hidden by it; models in your LM Studio always pass.

    


    
The detail panel (right)

    

      
Top of the panel
The answer first: the verdict, the best file for this PC, the file you have in LM Studio (if any), and one action: Load in LM Studio… or Choose a file to download. The star button (☆) at the right stars the model as a favorite. Below the name: the labels, and the two key facts (parameters and maximum context). The license (with "(gated)" or "(not gated)"), release date and downloads are at the top of the Overview tab, and a line Name says when the name has a word like abliterated, Heretic, base or distill (see "Words in model names"). This part stays in view while you scroll; it shrinks to the name, the answer and the tabs. A very long name takes at most 3 lines and its Hugging Face link 1 line; point at them for the full text.

      
Panel width
Drag the thin line at the left edge of the detail panel to make it wider or narrower (or click it and use the arrow keys). Double-click the line for the normal width (a little narrower on screens below 1500 px). The width is remembered in this browser. The list always keeps room for all its columns, so on a small screen (1366 px) the panel can grow only a little.

      
Overview
One quiet line (runs on, estimated or measured), then speed, memory, best file and context, and "Which file fits?" for the quantizations Q8_0 to Q2_K (the verdict itself is at the top of the panel). When llmfit's planner (more safety room) gives the best file another verdict or another way to run than the list, an amber note says so and names the best file that runs well. Preview speed shows how fast text appears at that speed. "Why this result" starts with llmfit's own level (Perfect, Good, Marginal or Too tight) and lists llmfit's reasons.

      
KV cache from a real file
llmfit counts the memory for the context (the KV cache) exactly only when it knows a model's layers and head size. For about 4,900 models in its list it does not, and uses a rough rule. When TensorTailor reads a real GGUF file of such a model (your file in My models, or a file in the Files or Run tab), it fills in the missing values from the file's header. It never changes a value llmfit has, and skips a file that does not match. "Why this result" then says "KV cache counted from the GGUF header of …", and My models lists your models it did this for. Remembered in this browser.

      
Files
The real files on Hugging Face, one line per file (point at a long name for the full name; the copy button copies the download link), the file "best for this PC" first, and the verdict per file. Download saves a file into your folder and checks its SHA-256 at the end. Show SHA-256 of each file (under the table) shows the codes; click one to copy it. GGUF source (at the top) picks which Hugging Face copy you look at; when llmfit knows none, Find GGUF copies searches Hugging Face and Use takes one. The file list is kept 12 hours ("kept 12 h"). "Fits here" uses llmfit's planner with extra safety room, in short words: Tight = runs, but tight; Slower = runs, but slower. Tags per file: vision projector (needed for images), draft head (optional speed-up), part 2 of 3 (a split model: all parts are needed, Download takes them all), pickle file — can run code (.bin/.pt: use the GGUF instead); a line under the table says whether the repo has pickle files.

      
Run
"LM Studio load settings for this PC" (shown when LM Studio is chosen under "Run it on this PC" and the model fits): what to set in LM Studio's load dialog for this model (context length, GPU offload, flash attention, K/V cache, max concurrent predictions, speculative decoding, remember settings, and "All other fields": leave the defaults), with the reason; point at a ? for what each setting does. Memory per context (folded, under the card) lists llmfit's memory estimate per context length with KV F16 and Q8_0; a number in the Won't fit colour does not fit in your VRAM. Set it by hand in LM Studio (folded) says where these settings are in LM Studio. Load in LM Studio with these settings (when the model is in LM Studio and its server is on) first shows what will be sent, then asks LM Studio to load it: context length, flash attention, KV cache on the GPU and MTP. LM Studio's API cannot set GPU offload and K/V cache quantization, so LM Studio keeps its own setting for those. A model that is loaded already is unloaded first; other loaded models stay, unless you tick the box to unload them. Run it on this PC gives the same as commands: the LM Studio command uses the card's context, and "--gpu max" only when the model fits fully in VRAM; the llama.cpp command puts all layers on the GPU only then (a MoE model that does not fit keeps its experts in system RAM with --cpu-moe).

      
Run: Run it on this PC
Ready commands for LM Studio, llama.cpp or Ollama, to paste into PowerShell. A name with unusual characters goes in single quotes, so nothing in it can run; a name that cannot be made safe (for example one that starts with "-") gets no command. With LM Studio, a model you already have shows your file first ("Your file in LM Studio · quant Q4_K_S"); llmfit's best file is named below it as an option: higher quality but less room for context, or smaller but lower quality. Below it: settings for AI agents (OpenCode, Codex).

      
Plan
llmfit's planner: the minimum and recommended hardware, which routes are possible here (GPU only, GPU + system RAM, MoE offload (experts in RAM), CPU only) with a verdict and speed for each, and What would make it better?: for example "+1.7 GB VRAM → Runs well (llmfit: Good)". Below: whether a target speed is reached (the answer uses the route speeds above), how many chats fit at the same time, and the disk space for your best models.

    


    
Views (top) and keys

    

      
Version (top left)
Under the name TensorTailor: the version of this page, then llmfit's version. A release from GitHub says its release tag, for example v2026.11.17 (a second release on one day: v2026.11.17-2); a newer release has a later date. Compare it with the newest one on the repo's Releases page. A page built on a PC says dev build and its commit. About (Tools menu) shows the version, the build date and the commit.

      
Models · My models · Battle · Downloads
Click to switch, or press 1 to 4. The small numbers on the buttons: models in your LM Studio (My models), models picked for Battle, and downloads not finished yet (Downloads). Esc brings you back to Models; in a text box it first leaves the box, and an open menu or window closes first.

      
My models
The models in your LM Studio (its server must be on: Developer tab → server on; if it still shows nothing, switch on Enable CORS in the server settings), with their size on disk, whether they are loaded, the verdict and speed here. The speed is for your file: llmfit counts its own best file (often another quant), so its range is scaled to the size of your file (point at the speed for both). On a narrow screen the Arch column is left out (MTP still shows under State). Show opens the model in Models. "bundled with LM Studio" marks the embedding model that comes with LM Studio itself (it is not in your models folder; LM Studio uses it to search documents you add to a chat).

      
My models: your files
My models asks once for LM Studio's models folder (the same folder as for Downloads). TensorTailor matches each model to its file by size and reads only the start of the file. The Arch column then shows the layers and the maximum context ("65 layers · max 256K"); point at it for the KV cache figures (KV heads, head size, how many layers keep the whole context). For models that TensorTailor added itself ("live" or "from LM Studio"), llmfit then counts the KV cache exactly instead of roughly. When LM Studio does not name the quant, the file name tells it.

      
My models: MTP labels
MTP on / MTP off: LM Studio's own setting for a loaded model. MTP in file: the header of your model file has MTP layers. MTP (name): only the name says MTP; read your files to know for sure. "no MTP in file": the name says MTP, the file has none.

      
My models: summary cards
Five cards at the top: Models (and how many files TensorTailor read from your folder), On disk, Updates (files with a newer version on Hugging Face), Similar (space of models made from the same base model, and how much keeping one per group would free) and Measured (your real speed as a share of llmfit's estimate). The details are in the lines above the table and in the rows.

      
My models: Best setting
For a model matched to llmfit's list: the longest context that runs well, from llmfit's planner, the same advice as the LM Studio card in the Run tab ("Runs well up to 32K · 64K with KV Q8_0" = 32K normally, 64K with K and V cache at Q8_0). Capped at LM Studio's maximum. Not matched: a rough estimate from the file size. A loaded model with a longer context than fits gets a warning.

      
My models: Updates
After TensorTailor has read your models folder, it asks Hugging Face about each model file and its vision projector (the folder name is the repo; once a day, or Check again; a check that got no answer is tried again after 10 minutes). The card says "All up to date" only when every file was checked and is the same; else "No update found". update available: the file on Hugging Face now has another size or SHA-256; What changed ↗ opens its history. maybe changed: same size, but changed there after your file's date. "✓ up to date (size and date)": same size, not changed since. Check SHA-256 reads your whole file from disk (about a minute per 10 GB) and compares it exactly; then it says "(SHA-256, checked here)". Files you got with Downloads were already checked at the end of the download: "(SHA-256, checked at download)", no button needed. The update check never replaces or deletes a file.

      
My models: Measure
Next to a model that is loaded in LM Studio. LM Studio writes about 256 tokens (10–30 seconds) and TensorTailor keeps the real speed next to the estimate: "measured 27 · est. 30–40 for your Q4_K_M", also in the detail panel's Speed. Point at it for the details (date, context, first token, the estimate then). The estimate is llmfit's, scaled to your own file when llmfit counts another quant. Measure never loads or unloads a model: before each request it asks LM Studio again whether the model is still loaded, and stops if not. With GPU measurements of models in llmfit's list (MTP off: speculative decoding is faster than any estimate), other models show "your PC: ~A–B" in the detail panel: llmfit's range times your measured share (the median).

      
My models: Similar models and disk space
Below the list: your models that come from the same base model (fine-tunes, uncensored versions, other quants), with their size on disk, verdict, best setting and folder (click to copy). Hugging Face says per repo which model it was made from; TensorTailor follows that up to the first model (remembered 30 days, Check again asks anew). It starts at the repo of the model's folder, else at the matched model (also a "from LM Studio" row named after its repo). Without an answer, the same design in the file header groups them. "Keeping only one of them would free up to …" = the group minus its smallest model. The label 3 similar in the My models table marks them. TensorTailor never deletes anything: remove a model in LM Studio or in File Explorer.

      
Battle
2 to 4 models (checkbox in the list, or Add to battle in Top picks) compete on this PC in four rounds: quality, speed, memory and context. One winner is named; with the same rounded points it says "a tie" and names the winner on the decimals. These battle points are not llmfit's Score (list, Compare), so the order can differ.

      
Selected · Compare
Tick the box of a model (or press C), at most 4. A strip Selected: appears at the bottom with their names (a long name ends in "…"; point at it for the full name; × removes one). Compare opens the window "Compare models" with them side by side (2 or more); Battle opens the Battle view; Clear removes all ticks.

      
Downloads
Files you download (tab Files → Download). Choose LM Studio's models folder once; each file goes to the publisher's and repo's folder, so LM Studio sees it at once. Files come in parts, so Resume goes on after a crash. The SHA-256 of the parts is checked before they are joined, so a damaged download never appears in LM Studio's folder; at the end the joined file is read back from disk and checked again (Check again reads it once more), and only then are the parts removed. If the joined file differs, TensorTailor removes it and joins once more from the checked parts; if it differs again (a memory or disk problem on this PC), it is removed and the parts stay. SHA-256 differs (red bar) for a file that was already in the folder: Download again asks you, deletes it and downloads it anew. Remove during a download stops it and removes its parts. Download again first asks Hugging Face for the file's newest size and SHA-256 (a new version there is not called "damaged"). A download remembers the folder it went into: after Change folder, Resume, Check again and Download again ask you to choose that folder again. A different file with the same name is never overwritten. A vision model's projector (mmproj) is taken along, and all parts of a split model ("part 1 of 3") are downloaded together. Remove from list never deletes a finished model file. When a file cannot be deleted because it is in use (LM Studio has the model loaded), the message says so: eject the model in LM Studio, then try again. Two TensorTailor tabs never work on the same file: the second tab leaves it waiting and says so (press Resume there when the first tab is done); Remove, Check again and Download again also leave a file alone that another tab works on. Before it starts, a tab reads the download list again, so a file another tab finished or removed is shown as such. Remove waits while Check again or Download again is busy with the same file.

      
Favorites ★
Click the star next to a model (or press W) to make it a favorite. Only my favorites (filters) shows just those. My models shows the same stars for your LM Studio models that are matched to llmfit's list; click a star there to add or remove it too.

      
Token key (top right)
The key button left of the question mark: grey with no Hugging Face token, green with a small dot when a token is set. Click it to set, change or remove the token (only needed for gated models; it stays in this browser tab).

      
Tools (top right)
The button with three lines (in the open menu, the arrow keys, Home and End move between the items; Esc closes it). Catalog: where the list comes from, and "Fetch new models" from Hugging Face. Hugging Face token: set / not set: the same window as the key button (a read-only token for gated models; it stays only in this browser tab). Light or dark (key T). About and licenses.

      
Keys
/ search · ↑ ↓ (or j k) move · C select for compare · W favorite (star) · T light or dark · ? help. With Tab the list is one stop: then the arrow keys move in it, Enter jumps to the detail panel and Space selects for compare (like C). The next Tab goes on to the Selected: strip (when you picked models) and then the detail panel. A screen reader hears the chosen row, and what C and W did.
